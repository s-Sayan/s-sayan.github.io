The sensor has a fixed size; focal length sets how far behind the lens it sits. Rays from the sensor edges through the lens center bound the field:

FOV = 2 · arctan(d / 2f)

Short f: the sensor accepts a wide cone and each object covers few pixels. Long f: the same pixels share a narrow patch, so each object covers more of them. Field of view and magnification are one piece of geometry.

Plate scale is 206265 / f arcsec per unit length on the focal plane.

The convex secondary intercepts the beam before the prime focus and slows its convergence. With the prime focus as a virtual object:

p = f₁ − d, s = d + b
m = s / p, F = m · f₁

Extending the final ray back to the incoming ray's height places an equivalent single lens a distance F in front of the focus. Plate scale is 206265 / F and FOV ≈ detector size × plate scale, so each side of the field shrinks by 1/m and the area by 1/m².

  • Larger m puts the secondary nearer the prime focus, where the beam is narrower, so the secondary is smaller.
  • Focus moves by about m² + 1 times any secondary motion: at m ≈ 4, 10 µm of despace shifts focus by about 170 µm.
  • These are paraxial numbers. Coma and field curvature set the usable field; mirror conics or a corrector address those without changing F.
